# Lesson 5: Publishing and Sharing

## Why Publish?

Publishing your dashboards to Tableau Public serves two purposes:

1. **Portfolio.** Potential employers can see your work. Many data analyst job applications ask for Tableau samples — a Tableau Public profile is the easiest way to show them.

2. **Sharing.** Anyone with the link can view and interact with your dashboard. No Tableau installation needed — it runs in a web browser.

## How to Publish

### Step 1: Save to Tableau Public

1. Go to **File** > **Save to Tableau Public As...**
2. Sign in with your Tableau Public account (create one if you haven't)
3. Give your workbook a descriptive name (e.g., "Sales Analysis Dashboard")
4. Click **Save**

Tableau uploads your workbook, including the data, to the Tableau Public servers. This takes a moment depending on your connection and data size.

### Step 2: View Your Published Dashboard

After saving, Tableau opens your browser to show the published dashboard. The URL will look something like:

```
public.tableau.com/views/YourWorkbookName/DashboardName
```

This is your dashboard's public URL. Anyone can view it.

### Step 3: Test the Interactivity

- Click around on the published version
- Test that filters work
- Check that tooltips appear on hover
- Make sure everything looks the same as in the desktop version

## Your Tableau Public Profile

Your profile page shows all your published workbooks. To find it:

1. Go to **public.tableau.com**
2. Click your profile icon (top right)
3. Select **My Profile**

Your profile URL is: `public.tableau.com/app/profile/yourname`

This is the link you'd include on a resume or LinkedIn profile.

## Updating a Published Dashboard

If you make changes to a published workbook:

1. Make your edits in Tableau Public on your desktop
2. Go to **File** > **Save to Tableau Public As...**
3. Select the existing workbook name (or use the same name)
4. Click **Save**

The published version updates automatically.

## Embedding in Other Websites

You can embed your dashboard on a personal website, blog, or portfolio:

1. Open your published dashboard on Tableau Public
2. Click the **Share** button (bottom of the dashboard)
3. Copy the **Embed Code** (HTML)
4. Paste the code into your website's HTML

## Best Practices for Your Portfolio

### Choose Good Projects

- Use real-world datasets (the external datasets from Module 4 are perfect)
- Answer interesting questions ("What factors predicted Titanic survival?" is more engaging than "Here's a bar chart")
- Show a variety of chart types across different workbooks

### Write Descriptions

When you save a workbook, add a description:
1. Go to your Tableau Public profile
2. Click on a workbook
3. Click **Edit Details**
4. Add a description explaining:
   - What question you're answering
   - What data you used
   - Any interesting findings

### Keep It Clean

- 3-5 polished dashboards are better than 20 messy ones
- Remove any "Sheet 1" or "test" worksheets before publishing
- Double-check that titles, labels, and formatting are professional

## Privacy Note

Remember: everything on Tableau Public is **public**. Do not upload:
- Personal data (your own finances, health records)
- Confidential work data
- Anything with real people's private information

The datasets in this course are all public and safe to use.
