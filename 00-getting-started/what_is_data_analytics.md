# What Is Data Analytics?

## You Already Do This

Have you ever looked at your bank statement and noticed you spend more on coffee in winter? That is data analytics.

Have you ever compared prices at two grocery stores to figure out which one is cheaper? That is data analytics too.

Have you ever checked the weather forecast before deciding what to wear? Believe it or not, that forecast was created using data analytics.

Data analytics is something you already do naturally. The only difference is that in this course, you will learn how to do it with a computer — which lets you work with much more data, much faster, and much more accurately than you could by hand.

---

## What Is "Data"?

Data is just information. That is it. Any collection of facts or observations counts as data. Here are some everyday examples:

- The list of transactions on your bank statement
- The grades on a school report card
- The number of steps your phone tracks each day
- The temperatures recorded every hour by a weather station
- A spreadsheet of sales numbers at a small business

Data can be numbers, text, dates, categories — anything that describes something about the world.

---

## What Does "Analytics" Mean?

Analytics means examining data to find patterns, answer questions, and make better decisions. When you look at your bank statement and notice the coffee spending pattern, you are examining data (your transactions) to find a pattern (more coffee in winter) that helps you make a decision (maybe bring coffee from home to save money).

In a work setting, analytics answers questions like:

- "Which of our products sells the most in December?"
- "Are customers leaving our service, and if so, why?"
- "Where should we open our next store?"
- "Is this new marketing campaign actually working?"

---

## Why Is Data Analytics Valuable?

Decisions based on data tend to be better than decisions based on gut feelings alone. Here is why businesses and organizations care so much about it:

**It saves money.** A hospital might analyze patient data and discover that a certain procedure leads to fewer follow-up visits, saving thousands of dollars per patient.

**It makes money.** A store might notice that customers who buy diapers also tend to buy baby wipes, so they place them next to each other and sales go up.

**It saves time.** Instead of a manager guessing which employees need more training, data can show exactly where mistakes happen most.

**It helps people.** City governments use analytics to figure out where to put new bus routes, which neighborhoods need more streetlights, and where potholes are most likely to form.

## What Jobs Use Data Analytics?

Data analytics is used in almost every industry. Here are some real examples:

- **Data Analyst** — The most direct role. You spend your days exploring data, creating charts and reports, and helping your team make decisions based on evidence.
- **Marketing Analyst** — You figure out which ads are working, which social media posts get the most engagement, and where to spend the advertising budget.
- **Financial Analyst** — You analyze revenue, expenses, and investment performance to help a company manage its money.
- **Healthcare Analyst** — You look at patient records, treatment outcomes, and hospital operations to improve care and reduce costs.
- **Business Intelligence Analyst** — You build dashboards and reports that help executives see what is happening across the company at a glance.
- **Operations Analyst** — You find ways to make processes more efficient, like reducing delivery times or cutting waste in a factory.

You do not need a specific degree for many of these roles. What you need are the skills — and that is exactly what this course will teach you.

## What Will You Learn in This Course?

By the end of this course, you will know how to:

1. **Collect data** — Get data from files, databases, and other sources.
2. **Clean data** — Fix messy, incomplete, or inconsistent data so it is ready to analyze.
3. **Analyze data** — Use Python and SQL to explore data, calculate statistics, and find patterns.
4. **Visualize data** — Create charts and graphs that make your findings easy to understand.
5. **Communicate findings** — Present your results clearly so other people can act on them.

These are the core skills that employers look for in a data analyst.

---

## You Do Not Need to Be a Math Genius

If math was not your favorite subject in school, do not worry. Most day-to-day data analytics uses basic arithmetic — adding, subtracting, averages, and percentages. The computer does the heavy lifting. Your job is to ask the right questions and interpret the results.
