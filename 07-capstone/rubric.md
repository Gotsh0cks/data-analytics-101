# Capstone Rubric

Use this rubric to decide whether your capstone is ready to share. It is not meant to make every project look the same. A simple project can be finished, useful, and worth sharing.

## Rating Levels

| Level | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| Finished | Good enough to stop polishing. The project is understandable, reproducible, and ready to share with a friend, mentor, or future-you. |
| Strong | Clear and polished. The project has the required pieces plus stronger explanations, cleaner visuals, or better organization. |
| Stretch | Beyond the course baseline. The project adds thoughtful extras without making the work confusing. |

Aim for **Finished** in every category before you keep polishing. Strong and Stretch are nice, but Finished is the win condition.

## Rubric Categories

| Category | Finished | Strong | Stretch |
|----------|----------|--------|---------|
| Dataset chosen and loaded | You chose a dataset and can load it from your project files. | You explain why the dataset fits your project question. | You include source notes, data dictionary notes, or refresh instructions. |
| Questions | You wrote 3-5 focused questions that can be answered with the data. | The questions build on each other and point toward a clear story. | You separate primary questions from optional follow-up questions. |
| Cleaning notes | You did basic cleaning or explained why cleaning was not needed. | You explain missing values, duplicates, data types, or columns you skipped. | You compare results before and after an important cleaning choice. |
| Analysis correctness | Your calculations answer the questions and use appropriate grouping, filtering, or sorting. | You double-check at least one result with another method or sanity check. | You add one useful deeper comparison, such as a segment, trend, or ranked table. |
| Findings | You state at least 3 findings in plain English and include numbers when helpful. | Your findings are ordered from most important to least important. | You connect findings to a practical recommendation or next question. |
| Charts | You created at least 3 charts with readable titles, labels, and file names. | The charts support the findings and avoid clutter. | You improve readability with annotations, ordering, color choices, or cleaner formatting. |
| SQL step | You attempted a SQL step, or you clearly explain why SQL was not useful for this project. | Your SQL result supports one finding or data check. | You combine SQL with Python, Excel, or Tableau in a useful way. |
| Dashboard or presentation | You created a Tableau dashboard or an alternative presentation of your findings. | The dashboard or presentation is easy to scan and has a clear title. | You add a filter, highlight, or layout choice that helps the reader explore. |
| Summary | You wrote a 3-5 sentence summary of what you found. | The summary includes the main finding, evidence, and one limitation. | The summary also names a next step or a decision someone could make from the work. |
| Reproducibility | Someone else can find the files and rerun or follow the main steps. | Your folders, file names, and instructions are easy to follow. | You include a short run command, output list, or notes for refreshing the project. |

## Ready To Share

Your project is ready to share when every category reaches **Finished**. That means you can stop polishing even if a chart could be prettier or a question could be deeper.

If you want to improve after that, pick one category and move it from Finished to Strong. Do not try to improve everything at once.
