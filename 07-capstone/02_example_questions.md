# Example Questions by Dataset

Not sure what to analyze? Here are starter questions for each of the external datasets. Pick 3-5 that interest you.

---

## Titanic (`data/external/titanic.csv`)

1. What was the overall survival rate?
2. How did survival rate differ by passenger class (1st, 2nd, 3rd)?
3. Were women more likely to survive than men?
4. What was the average age of survivors vs. non-survivors?
5. Did having family aboard (SibSp, Parch) affect survival?
6. How much did ticket fare vary by class?
7. Which port of embarkation (C, Q, S) had the highest survival rate?

---

## Restaurant Tips (`data/external/tips.csv`)

1. What is the average tip percentage (tip / total_bill)?
2. Which day of the week has the highest average tip percentage?
3. Do larger parties tip a higher or lower percentage?
4. Is there a difference in tipping between lunch and dinner?
5. Do smokers tip differently than non-smokers?
6. What is the relationship between bill size and tip amount?

---

## World Happiness (`data/external/world_happiness.csv`)

1. Which are the top 10 happiest countries?
2. Which region of the world is happiest on average?
3. What factor (GDP, social support, freedom, etc.) correlates most with happiness?
4. Are wealthier countries always happier?
5. Which countries score high on freedom but low on happiness (or vice versa)?

---

## Iris Flowers (`data/external/iris.csv`)

1. How do the three species differ in average petal length?
2. Which species has the widest range of sepal widths?
3. Is there a relationship between petal length and petal width?
4. Can you visually separate the species using just two measurements?
5. Which two measurements are most useful for telling species apart?

---

## Tips for Choosing Questions

- **Start broad, then narrow.** "What affects survival?" is too broad. "Does passenger class affect survival rate?" is focused.
- **Make them answerable.** You need data to answer the question. "Why did the Titanic sink?" is not answerable from passenger data.
- **Mix question types.** Include at least one comparison (A vs. B), one trend or distribution, and one relationship (X vs. Y).
- **Think about the audience.** Who would care about your findings? A manager? A researcher? A journalist?
